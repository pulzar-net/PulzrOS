# RS485 and Modbus

The RAK7289CV2 (WisGate Edge Pro) has a Modbus RTU bus on the board, and it
does more than carry readings: on the newer hardware revision the power board's
watchdog sits on it, and a unit on DC power whose OS does not feed that
watchdog loses its power. `parkeze-rs485` owns the bus. It feeds the watchdog,
reads the battery, polls the Modbus devices configured in UCI, and reports to
parkeze.net.

## What is known, and from where

Nothing here has been seen on our bench. There is no RS485 device to test
with, and the bench Pro's watchdog has not been observed answering. The design
rests on two sources:

- **ChirpStack's RAK support**, the one known-working user of this bus:
  `chirpstack/chirpstack-gateway-utils`, `src/rak/watchdog.rs` and
  `src/rak/battery.rs`. They use Modbus RTU at 9600 8N1, with a 100 ms timeout
  per request.
  - Slave `0xC8` (200) is the watchdog. Writing `10` to holding register
    `0x0001` feeds it.
  - Slave `0x6E` (110) is the battery. ChirpStack reads four holding
    registers, one at a time: `0x6000` voltage ×0.01 V, `0x6003` health %,
    `0x6005` remaining capacity, `0x6006` full-charge capacity.
- **chirpstack/chirpstack-gateway-os#155.** RAK support says the newer
  RAK7289V2/CV2 revision has a hardware watchdog and "fully reboots every 10
  minutes" unless WisGateOS feeds it. The reporter saw a unit on 12 V DC go off
  after about ten minutes and stay off, and saw no problem on PoE. ChirpStack
  feeds every 5 minutes from cron, and the reporter confirmed that keeps a DC
  unit up. The dmesg in that issue, from a RAK7289V2, shows the bridge:
  `ch341 1-1.4:1.0: ch341-uart converter detected`, so a CH340 (`1a86:7523`)
  at port `1-1.4` on the internal hub. RAK's instructions there for
  disabling the watchdog name its WisGateOS daemon, `spregread`.

Still open:

| Question | What depends on it |
|---|---|
| Does a CV2 under ParkezeOS show the CH340 at `1-1.4`? | `PARKEZE_RS485` in `boards/rak7289cv2/board.conf`. A different port means the daemon reads `absent` and feeds nothing. |
| Do the watchdog and battery answer at 200 and 110? | Everything here. ChirpStack's code is the only evidence. |
| Is the RS485 pair on the 4-pin DC connector this same bus? | Whether external devices go here. The design assumes one shared bus. If the connector is a separate port, the external devices are not reachable through this one, and only the watchdog and battery are. |
| Does the bus need RS485 direction control from the host? | Nothing changes yet. ChirpStack sets none (tokio-serial in plain mode), so neither does this: libmodbus stays in its RS232 mode, which assumes the transceiver switches direction itself. |
| What does register `0x0001 = 10` mean? | The feed interval. It may be a timeout in minutes. Feeding every two minutes is safe under any reading that keeps ChirpStack's five working. |
| What does the DC watchdog do during a long sysupgrade, or in failsafe or recovery? | Nothing feeds it in those states. A flash takes about four minutes on this board and the last feed is at most two minutes before it, so it should fit inside ten. Recovery on DC power will not fit. |

## Design

**One owner, one request at a time.** On a shared bus, the watchdog, the
battery and anything wired to the DC connector are all slaves of one master.
Two masters on one RS485 pair corrupt each other's frames. So one process
opens the port, and nothing else does:

- The MQTT agent's `rs485` op prints the daemon's record. It does not read the
  bus.
- The ModemManager tty hotplug override (`boards/common/files/etc/hotplug.d/tty/25-modemmanager-tty`)
  skips the bridge's tty, so ModemManager never writes AT probes onto the bus.

**The port is found by USB path, not by number.** On a CV2 the EG95 modem
takes four ttyUSB nodes. Which of the modem and the CH340 is `ttyUSB0`
depends on which driver binds first, and ChirpStack hardcodes
`/dev/ttyUSB0`. The daemon looks under
`/sys/bus/usb/devices/1-1.4/1-1.4:1.0/` for the tty, checks the vid:pid, and
looks again whenever the node goes away.

**The watchdog comes first.**

- It is fed every 120 s. After a failure it is retried every 15 s. The
  watchdog allows ten minutes, so this survives four missed feeds where
  ChirpStack's cron survives one.
- Between every two jobs the scheduler checks whether the watchdog is due. A
  slow device can delay a feed by one device's poll, never by more.
- A device that times out on one register is not asked for the rest of that
  poll, because each register would cost another full timeout.
- A config error in one device drops that device and is reported. It never
  stops the feed.
- The init script starts the daemon at `START=15`, with no gate on adoption,
  network or broker, and respawns it with no limit.

**Watchdog states.**

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `fed` | The last feed succeeded. |
| `failing` | It answered earlier in this boot and has stopped. On DC power the unit is ten minutes from a power cut. Reported at once. |
| `absent` | It has never answered: a revision without the watchdog, or a bus that does not reach it. These two cannot be told apart from here. |

**The battery** is an optional accessory, so a slave that has never answered
is `absent` and is asked every 30 minutes instead of every 5.

**Telling parkeze.net.** The record rides `parkeze-claim report` as
`facts.rs485`, the same report that carries the rest of the gateway's health.
That report goes every six hours. The daemon also asks for one when the
summary changes:

- the port state
- the watchdog state
- the battery state, or its charge crossing 50, 20 or 10 %
- a device's state

Those reports are at most one every five minutes. The first settled state is
not one of them, because the report loop's own first report goes 30 seconds
after boot. Values that only drift (a voltage, a meter reading) wait for the
six-hourly report, or for Refresh on the gateway page, which asks over MQTT.

**Times are ages.** The record says `fedAgo`, not a timestamp. The gateway's
clock is 1970 until NTP answers, and the watchdog is fed from the first seconds
of boot. parkeze.net counts back from when the record arrived.

## Why Lua

The task was a maintained Modbus library rather than hand-rolled RTU framing
and CRC. The one OpenWrt packages is libmodbus 3.1.8, and its bindings are:

| Binding | Size on mipsel | Usable here |
|---|---|---|
| `lua-libmodbus` 0.7 | 8 KB, plus `liblua` (64 KB) | Yes |
| `python3-libmodbus` | needs `python3-light`, megabytes | No: does not fit a 16 MB board |
| ucode | none exists | No: writing one means compiling a package per architecture, which this feed does not do |

So the daemon is Lua 5.1. libmodbus does the framing, CRC, timeouts and error
recovery (`ERROR_RECOVERY_PROTOCOL`: flush and wait out the timeout after a bad
frame). The rest of the stack is also OpenWrt packages:

- `lua-cjson` writes the record
- `libuci-lua` reads UCI
- `libubox-lua`'s uloop runs the timer

The whole stack adds about 100 KB compressed.

Integers are put together from registers in Lua, not with lua-libmodbus's
`get_s16`/`get_s32`. Those convert out-of-range doubles through C casts, which
is undefined behaviour. Floats use the library's `get_f32`/`get_f32le`,
because reinterpreting the bits needs C.

Release images strip whole-line `--` comments from Lua files
(`scripts/strip-lua-comments.py`, called from `harden-payload.sh`). They do not
compile Lua to bytecode. That would need `luac` from OpenWrt's exact Lua build,
whose LNUM patch changes the bytecode format.

## Configuring a device

The bus settings live in `parkeze.rs485`: `baud`, `parity` (`none`, `even` or
`odd`) and `stopbits`. They apply to everything on the bus, the watchdog
included. On a shared bus, moving off 9600 8N1 stops the feed, and the
watchdog then reads `failing`.

Each device is a `modbus` section:

```sh
uci add parkeze modbus
uci set parkeze.@modbus[-1].name='meter'
uci set parkeze.@modbus[-1].slave='1'
uci set parkeze.@modbus[-1].interval='60'      # seconds, 5-86400
uci set parkeze.@modbus[-1].timeout='500'      # ms per request, 50-5000
uci add_list parkeze.@modbus[-1].register='voltage:input:0x0000:u16:0.1:V'
uci add_list parkeze.@modbus[-1].register='energy:holding:40:u32le::kWh'
uci add_list parkeze.@modbus[-1].register='door:discrete:2'
uci commit parkeze
/etc/init.d/parkeze-rs485 restart
```

A register is `name:table:address[:type[:scale[:unit]]]`:

- **table:** `holding`, `input`, `coil` or `discrete`.
- **address:** the register number on the wire, from 0. It is not the
  40001-style number some datasheets print.
- **type:** `u16`, `s16`, `u32`, `s32`, `f32`, or `u32le`, `s32le`, `f32le`
  for low word first. A coil or discrete input reads 0 or 1 and takes no
  type.

**Limits.** At most 8 devices, 16 registers each and 64 in all. Those caps
keep the record inside the MQTT agent's 8 KB answer. A device is refused if
its slave address is the watchdog's or the battery's. `option enabled '0'`
keeps a device in UCI without polling it.

A problem with a device is logged, reported, and shown on the gateway page.
That device is skipped and nothing else stops.

## Reading it on the gateway

```sh
parkeze-rs485 status      # the record, ages in seconds
logread -e rs485          # port, watchdog and device state changes
```

## Tests

`scripts/test-rs485.sh` runs two halves.

**Unit half.** It drives the scheduler, the config and the decoding against
a fake bus whose answers and clock the test chooses. It covers:

- feed timing and retries
- `failing` versus `absent`
- a port that is missing, breaks or renumbers
- the battery's register order
- a slow device not starving the watchdog
- report rate-limiting
- finding the tty in a fake sysfs

**Simulated half.** It builds lua-libmodbus 0.7 from the tarball whose hash
is OpenWrt's `PKG_HASH`, and links it to the host's libmodbus. It then
talks over a pty pair to a pymodbus slave (`scripts/rs485-test/simulator.py`),
which is an independent framer and decoder. It checks:

- the watchdog write the simulated power board receives
- the battery and meter values over the wire, in every type and word order
- an exception
- a slave that is not there
- a watchdog that falls silent mid-run

`RS485_UNIT_ONLY=1` runs only the unit half, on a machine without a C
compiler or pymodbus.

What neither half covers is the hardware itself: the CH340 under this kernel,
real line timing, and the direction switching between the transceiver and the
bus.
